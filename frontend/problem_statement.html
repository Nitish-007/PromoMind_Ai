The Problem in Retail & Commerce Today

India's $800 Billion retail market is dominated by over 13 Million independent Kirana stores and local supermarkets. Today, they face an existential crisis.

Problem 01

Asymmetry Against Quick-Commerce Giants

Venture-backed quick-commerce companies (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) and e-commerce giants use multi-million dollar algorithmic demand forecasting, dynamic basket bundling, and predictive delivery dark stores. Local retailers rely on handwritten books, gut instinct, and distributor push.

🚨 22% of neighborhood Kiranas report declining footfall due to quick-commerce discounting.
Problem 02

Trapped Working Capital & Dead Inventory

Retailers overstock slow-moving goods (e.g., premium biscuits, dry fruits) while running out of high-velocity anchors right before festival surges. Without mathematical velocity tracking, 15% to 25% of working capital remains frozen on dusty shelves, risking expiry and total write-off.

💰 ₹40,000 to ₹1,50,000 in cash is trapped per store in dead, non-moving inventory.
Problem 03

Offer Cannibalization & Margin Destruction (Offer Conflicts)

When retailers attempt promotions, they do so manually without conflict tracking. Discounting an item that is already under a distributor promotion or running simultaneous promotions on the same SKU destroys gross margins (cutting a 18% margin down to 3% or negative cash).

📉 Over 60% of manual grocery promotions result in net financial loss due to double discounting.
Problem 04

Multi-Brand Decision Blind Spots

When distributors push competing brands (e.g., Amul Pure Ghee vs Nandini vs Mother Dairy, or Fortune Oil vs Saffola), retailers lack side-by-side data. They cannot evaluate which brand gives higher customer recall vs which gives higher profit margins to stock the optimal mix.

🔍 Retailers lose 2-4% in profit margins by over-indexing on low-margin distributor push brands.
Problem 05

Hyper-Local Cultural & Regional Nuances

Festive grocery demand in India is extreme and deeply localized. In South India, Deepavali requires huge surges (+140%) in Clay Deepas/Diyas, Pure Cow Ghee, and Cotton Wicks, whereas normal months have near-zero demand (<5 units/month). National enterprise software ignores local cultural calendars.

🪔 Severe stockouts occur on regional festive days, followed by overstocking on normal days.
Problem 06

Omnichannel Marketing Execution Gap

Almost every Kirana has 1,000 to 2,000 neighborhood customers on WhatsApp. But store owners do not have the graphic design skills or copywriting tools to craft appealing deals, WhatsApp broadcasts, or printed shelf posters in seconds.

⏱️ 10+ hours wasted weekly on manual signage and haphazard messaging.
